02 Generative core

It doesn't come pre-loaded with someone else's business.

There is no ninety-day configuration project, because the model is reading the same records your team is. Your stage names, your product vocabulary, your quoting habits, the way your customers actually answer — that is the training material, and it is already in the system.

Herbie works today. The rest of the desk is coming.

An agent here owns a lane of the business and works between the records — not a chat window you have to remember to open, but staff who notice things and come to you. Sales ships at launch; the other three lanes are in build.

They ask before they act. Every time.

An agent that can write to your customers is only useful if you trust it, so Attaché asks for one capability at a time and you can say no to any of them. Nothing is switched on by default. Nothing reads your mail unless you say so. And you can see the full list of what each one is allowed to do, on one page, at any time.

You can rename him, too. Most people keep Herbie.

06 Permission

Your data trains your Attaché. Nobody else's.

What the platform learns about your business stays inside your business. Every tenant sits in its own isolated schema; agents act only on capabilities you have explicitly switched on; and every action an agent takes is written to an audit trail with your name on it.

Isolation

Your own schema

One tenant, one isolated set of tables. No shared row space, no “customer_id” filter standing between you and somebody else's data.

Consent

Nothing on by default

Each agent capability is granted one at a time, is visible on a single page, and can be revoked there the same way.

Accountability

Every action logged

Agent work is recorded like a person's — who asked, what changed, when — so an audit is a query, not an archaeology project.
